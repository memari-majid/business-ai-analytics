# State of the art in AI and business analytics

**Evidence reviewed November 18, 2026.** This snapshot focuses on research published or released since January 2025. It distinguishes experiments in firms, observational studies, and evaluations of systems. The [full research references](REFERENCES.md) give publication details. The [recent resource shortlist](RECENT_RESOURCES.md) covers books, courses, and guides. For a specific problem, use the [topic index](../learning/TOPIC_INDEX.md). ## What is established and what is advancing

| Area | Established practice | Research frontier | What to verify for a real decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data and business intelligence** | Define grain, join keys, time windows, and metric denominators. Reconcile dashboard measures against source records. | Copilots combine SQL, calculations, and retrieval across tables and documents. The DataSpace benchmark scores whether data agents return a complete, verifiable tabular result from a heterogeneous workspace. | Test which metric, period, and entities an assistant selected. Verify intermediate counts and trace important figures to their source. [Star-schema guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/guidance/star-schema) · [DataSpace preprint](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.03451) |
| **Tabular prediction** | Use interpretable rules or logistic models as baselines, then compare tree ensembles or other learners on a realistic holdout. Evaluate calibration and the decision threshold as well as ranking. | Recent benchmarks test pretrained tabular models against tuned tree models. The TabPFN-3.5 authors report comparisons that include temporal and grouped data splits. | Use a holdout reflecting the future population and the data available at decision time. Compare cost-weighted errors and how gains change across cohorts. [TabPFN study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08328-6) · [TabPFN-3.5 technical report, September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17895) · [Decision economics references](BOOKS.md#business-decisions-and-graduate-analytics) |
| **Forecasting and optimization** | Compare seasonal baselines and tuned models on historical forecast origins. Use uncertainty and asymmetric costs to choose an order, allocation, or staffing level. | Multivariate time-series foundation models can use related series and external inputs. Contextual forecasting benchmarks examine whether models use forward-looking information such as planned events. | Use identical horizons and information for every model. Check calibration and evaluate the downstream policy on stockouts, costs, or service levels. [Chronos-2 follow-up preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41956) · [GIFT-Eval-CTX preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17794) · [Inventory lab](../labs/02_inventory_decision.py) |
| **Experiments and causal ML** | Use randomized assignment where feasible. Define the treatment, comparator, estimand, guardrails, and economic threshold before looking at the result. For observational designs, justify identification. | Researchers combine historical causal evidence with embeddings to evaluate new actions described in text. A recent digital experimentation roundtable discusses using experimental, observational, and synthetic evidence for different questions. | Put effect heterogeneity and support on the same footing as model fit. Validate on completely held-out treatments and collect new evidence for actions far from the ones observed. [Ellickson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437261476639) · [November 2026 roundtable preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16335) · [Campaign lab](../labs/01_campaign_decision.ipynb) |
| **Text, customer research, and retrieval** | Build an auditable classification or extraction baseline. Validate labels against human examples. For question answering, retrieve policy or document evidence that applies to the user and time period. | The Wharton–Petco case combines text and image analytics with retrieval for email drafting. A separate synthetic-respondent preprint found that aggregate agreement with human responses could mask weak respondent-level discrimination. | Match validation to the decision. Audit subgroup and item-level errors, source dates, claim support, and whether the system abstains when evidence is missing. [Wharton–Petco case](https://ai-analytics.wharton.upenn.edu/student-programs/analytics-accelerator/optimizing-petcos-email-marketing-strategy-using-generative-ai/) · [Netzer and Sambandam preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13995) · [Grounded Q&A lab](../labs/03_grounded_policy_qa.py) |
| **Agents and workflow evaluation** | Separate reading data, calculating, drafting, acting, and approving. Grade exact business checks in code where possible and review trade-offs separately. | Recent benchmarks evaluate work products and how an agent carries out a task. BizAgentBench records whether intermediate actions follow policy, and MBABench-2.0 tests the auditability and usefulness of financial spreadsheets. | Report full-task pass rates, failure categories, and results across repeated runs. A correct answer can coexist with bad actions or unsupported evidence. [BizAgentBench, November revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424v3) · [MBABench-2.0 preprint](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.31711) · [Agent evaluation guide](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) |
| **Business value and deployment** | Compare with the incumbent workflow. Account for integration and human review, operational errors, adoption, permissions, monitoring, and rollback. | Recent field experiments measure outcomes in operating businesses. Usage studies describe tasks and adoption. Governance research offers ways to design and document controls. | Measure outcomes on both sides of the decision, such as resolution and repeat contact. Track total costs, risk, who benefits, and whether results persist. [Research on organizations and work](RESEARCH.md#organizations-and-real-work) · [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) |

## November 2026 findings

- **Sales outcomes in a controlled comparison.** Fang et al. randomized interventions across seven workflows at one cross-border retailer. Short-run sales effects ranged from no detectable change to 16.3%, with gains mainly through conversion. Compare each workflow with its own incumbent. Estimate margin and rollout costs separately. [*Management Science*, November 6, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2026.00623)
- **How people and AI divide work.** Hosanagar and Ahn compared three collaboration designs in a creative-writing experiment. Completion times were similar. When AI led and people approved or rejected its output, quality, satisfaction, and creative diversity were lower. Use these outcomes to design a studio comparison of different approval and editing responsibilities. [*PNAS Nexus*, November 10, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag353)
- **Firm outcomes from a platform rollout.** Barwick et al. estimate 51.6% higher monthly sales after adoption among firms with annual sales below RMB 5 million on one Chinese e-commerce platform. This is a matched difference-in-differences estimate for a specific group of firms. The intervention, unit of analysis, and identifying assumptions differ from Fang et al.'s randomized workflow tests. [NBER working paper, November 2026](https://doi.org/10.3386/w36003)
- **Agent outcomes and acceptable execution.** Zhao et al. test 50 tasks based on payments workflows. In one reported model configuration, goal attainment was 42.0% and strict success was 32.7% when the rubric also required valid execution. For a studio project, grade the result, the applicable evidence, and the actions separately. [BizAgentBench preprint, November 5 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424v3)

## Related evidence from 2025 and 2026

- **Kim, Kim, and Koning, workflow redesign.** In a field experiment with 515 startups, the treatment group learned how other firms reorganized production around AI. The authors report increased discovery of AI use cases, with revenue gains concentrated toward the top of the distribution. The randomized comparison concerns guidance about where to apply AI within a program that gave firms access to AI tools. [INSEAD working paper, 2026](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6513481)
- **Poeltl et al., financial spreadsheets.** MBABench-2.0 tests 101 spreadsheet construction tasks and grades accuracy, auditability, readability, and maintainability. The best tested configuration passed 16% under the benchmark rubric. Use the criteria to assess a studio deliverable alongside its arithmetic. [MBABench-2.0 preprint, October 22, 2026](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.31711)
- **Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond, customer support.** A staggered rollout to 5,172 agents yielded a 15% average increase in issues resolved per hour, with substantial variation across workers. For a similar deployment, measure resolution quality and any work transferred to reviewers. [*The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044)
- **Dell’Acqua et al., consulting.** In a preregistered experiment with 758 consultants, GPT-4 improved speed and quality on selected tasks. On one difficult exercise, correctness was approximately 19 percentage points lower when pooling the GPT-4 arms against control. Figure 5 shows why a system needs checks for particular tasks and failure modes. [*Organization Science*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) · [Figure 5](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf#page=12)
- **Choi and Xie, accounting.** In a randomized framed experiment, 99 accountants classified 43 anonymized transactions. AI suggestions improved accuracy and efficiency on average, and less accurate suggestions could mislead participants. Separate survey and usage records describe how accountants use assistance in the field. [*Journal of Accounting Research*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.70052)
- **Tomitsuka et al., data agents.** On Argo-Bench, the strongest tested configuration earned at least 95/100 on 34.8% of 210 tasks. The study measures performance in a simulated food-delivery operation. Its error analysis includes cases where the agent used the wrong quantity or objective. [October 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02122#S3)
- **Patel et al., business cases.** On BusinessCaseBench, Claude Sonnet 4.6 earned 88.4% average rubric credit and met every criterion on 49.6% of questions. These are measures of model performance on supplied case questions. The scoring design is useful when deciding what a business recommendation must demonstrate. [BusinessCaseBench preprint, v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16057v5#S3)

## How this review was updated

The November 18 search used Scite for recent work on firm outcomes, marketing applications, and workflow benchmarks, then checked candidate DOIs and read available study text or primary abstracts. Recent entries were selected for their connection to a business decision, explicit comparison or evaluation design, and traceable results. The NBER working paper by Barwick et al. was checked at NBER because Scite had yet to index it when searched. The [research references](REFERENCES.md) identify publication types and stable links.

## Questions the evidence leaves open

- **Value outside the pilot.** Which gains survive broader adoption, a longer horizon, changing demand, and full implementation costs? Study the existing process and measure quality, work shifted to reviewers, and the cost of mistakes.
- **Transfer across populations.** Do a model's forecasts, recommendations, or estimated effects hold for new stores, customers, or policies? Choose holdouts that match the proposed change and monitor performance by cohort.
- **Delegation and controls.** Which steps should software execute and which should a person verify? Evaluate both the recommended decision and any actions the system can take.
- **Learning with AI.** When a student uses AI to write a case answer, can they defend its assumptions, recreate the key calculation, and revise their decision when new evidence arrives? Give those skills their own measures. See the [business-school teaching map](../learning/TEACHING_MAP.md).

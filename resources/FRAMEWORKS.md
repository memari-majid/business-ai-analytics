# Influential theories and frameworks

Some ideas explain why a business or its people behave as they do. Others specify an estimand, derive a decision rule from assumptions, describe an empirical pattern, or guide implementation. The type of idea determines what evidence to look for and what conclusions it can support.

## Framing and identifying the problem

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Primary source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CRISP-DM** · project methodology | Translate a business goal into an analytical question, then iterate through data understanding, preparation, modeling, evaluation, and deployment. Use it to assign deliverables and decide when an evaluation should send the team back to data preparation. | Teams still need to specify the causal design, production monitoring, and safeguards appropriate to the project. | [Chapman et al., CRISP-DM 1.0 (2000)](https://public.dhe.ibm.com/software/analytics/spss/documentation/modeler/14.2/fr/CRISP-DM.pdf) |
| **Explanation versus prediction** · statistical distinction | Choose the evaluation for the goal. A demand forecast needs an honest future holdout, while a claim that a price change caused the demand shift needs an identification strategy. | Predictive performance establishes how well a model predicts under the conditions of the test. An explanation depends on assumptions about the data-generating process. | [Shmueli, *To Explain or to Predict?* (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-STS330) |
| **Potential outcomes** · causal inference framework | Define effects by comparing outcomes under specified alternative actions for a population. An experiment can estimate average incremental purchases attributable to an offer. | For a given unit, only the outcome under the action taken is observed. Observational estimates depend on identifying assumptions such as exchangeability and overlap. | [Rubin, *Estimating Causal Effects of Treatments in Randomized and Nonrandomized Studies* (1974)](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-82c0-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b) |
| **Online controlled experiments** · experimental practice | Randomly assign eligible units, define outcomes and guardrails, and compare a new offer or workflow with the incumbent. Use a holdout or control to estimate the effect of assignment. | Interference, missing outcomes, invalid instrumentation, and repeated looks can undermine interpretation. Effects refer to the tested population, units, and time window. | [Kohavi et al., *Controlled Experiments on the Web* (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1) |
| **Heterogeneous effects and uplift** · causal modeling and targeting | Estimate how treatment effects vary with pre-treatment attributes. Causal forests are one method. In customer retention, compare a policy that targets estimated incremental response with an incumbent targeting rule. | Subgroup effect estimates can be noisy, and the original design still determines whether the effects are identified. Evaluate the resulting policy on separate data with costs and constraints. | [Wager and Athey, *Estimation and Inference of Heterogeneous Treatment Effects Using Random Forests* (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1319839) |

## Turning estimates into decisions

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Primary source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value of information** · decision theory | Estimate the improvement in expected value when a decision can use new information. Compare the benefit of buying data or running a pilot with its cost and the value lost while waiting. | The estimate depends on the available actions, outcome probabilities, consequences, and assumptions about how the evidence will influence action. | [Howard, *Information Value Theory* (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSSC.1966.300074) |
| **Predictive to prescriptive analytics** · optimization framework | Use features and predicted uncertainty to choose an action under a business objective and constraints. For staffing, evaluate the schedule it produces using labor cost and service performance. | The objective needs to reflect relevant consequences. Test decisions under realistic constraints and changes in demand. | [Bertsimas and Kallus, *From Predictive to Prescriptive Analytics* (2020 issue)](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3253) |
| **Newsvendor problem** · single-period inventory model | Choose an order quantity from the demand distribution by balancing shortage and overage costs. Under the standard linear cost model, the target demand quantile is shortage cost divided by the sum of shortage and overage costs. | The basic rule applies to a single period with the specified cost model. Replenishment during the period, shared capacity, or multi-period carryover changes the decision problem. | [Arrow, Harris, and Marschak, *Optimal Inventory Policy* (1951)](https://www.or.mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp/takeda/FreshmanCourse/ArrowHarrisMarschak.pdf) |

## Work, adoption, and governance

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Primary source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sociotechnical systems** · work-design theory | Study tools, roles, incentives, and coordination together. When adding AI to support work, examine who verifies drafts, handles exceptions, and bears additional review work. | The analysis depends on the actual work setting. Observe how the team adapts after deployment and measure both output quality and workload. | [Trist and Bamforth, *Some Social and Psychological Consequences of the Longwall Method of Coal-Getting* (1951)](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872675100400101) |
| **Technology acceptance model** · information-systems theory | Use perceived usefulness and ease of use to formulate adoption hypotheses. Measure those perceptions alongside sustained usage for a new analytics tool. | Acceptance and frequency of use are distinct from business value. Access requirements, incentives, and implementation can affect both. | [Davis, *Perceived Usefulness, Perceived Ease of Use, and User Acceptance of Information Technology* (1989)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/249008) |
| **Dynamic capabilities** · strategy theory | Examine how a firm senses opportunities, commits resources, and reconfigures routines. An AI pilot that works in one team may demand reusable data, oversight, and staff skills before it can scale. | Specify which capabilities are observable and what outcomes would show improvement. Changes in performance require separate evidence. | [Teece, *Explicating Dynamic Capabilities* (2007)](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/smj.640) |
| **Task-based economics of automation** · economic framework | Describe work as a bundle of tasks. Identify which tasks technology performs, where people remain complementary, and which new tasks emerge, such as verification or evaluation. | A task-level productivity gain is one input into labor and organizational outcomes. Demand, reassignment, and newly created tasks also matter. | [Acemoglu and Restrepo, *Automation and New Tasks* (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.33.2.3) |
| **Jagged technological frontier** · empirical finding and interpretive lens | Evaluate AI assistance at the task level. The field experiment found that consultants performed better on a set of tasks within the tested model's capabilities, and worse on a task selected outside them. | Where the boundary falls depends on the model, tools, users, and task. Test the actual workflow and its consequential failure cases. | [Dell’Acqua et al., *Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier* (2026 journal article)](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) |
| **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** · voluntary governance framework | Use Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage to assign owners, identify impacts, evaluate risks, and plan responses. Apply the generative AI profile to decisions about evidence, monitoring, and incidents. | The organization still has to define acceptable thresholds and demonstrate that its controls work in the use case. | [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/AI/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [Generative AI Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) |

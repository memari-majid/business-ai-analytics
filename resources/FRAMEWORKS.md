# Influential theories and frameworks

Some ideas explain how firms or people behave. Others specify an estimand, derive a decision rule, describe an empirical pattern, or guide implementation. The readings show how these ideas are taught or applied in sources published or updated since 2023.

## Framing and identifying the problem

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Recent reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CRISP-DM** · project methodology | Translate a business goal into an analytical question, then iterate through data understanding, preparation, modeling, evaluation, and deployment. Use it to assign deliverables and decide when an evaluation should send the team back to data preparation. | Teams still need to specify the causal design, production monitoring, and safeguards appropriate to the project. | [IBM, *CRISP-DM in SPSS Modeler*, version 19 documentation (2025)](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/spss-modeler/19.0.0?topic=overview-crisp-dm-in-spss-modeler) |
| **Explanation versus prediction** · statistical distinction | Choose the evaluation for the goal. A demand forecast needs an honest future holdout. A claim that a price change caused the demand shift needs an identification strategy. | Predictive performance establishes how well a model predicts under the conditions of the test. A causal explanation depends on assumptions about the data-generating process. | [Chernozhukov et al., *Applied Causal Inference Powered by ML and AI*, first two parts (2026 online update)](https://causalml-book.org/) |
| **Potential outcomes** · causal inference framework | Define effects by comparing outcomes under specified alternative actions for a population. An experiment can estimate average incremental purchases attributable to an offer. | For a given unit, only the outcome under the action taken is observed. Observational estimates depend on identifying assumptions such as exchangeability and overlap. | [Wager, *Causal Inference: A Statistical Learning Approach*, chapters 1–2 (2026 draft)](https://web.stanford.edu/~swager/causal_inf_book.pdf) |
| **Online controlled experiments** · experimental practice | Randomly assign eligible units, define outcomes and guardrails, and compare a new offer or workflow with the incumbent. Use a holdout or control to estimate the effect of assignment. | Interference, missing outcomes, invalid instrumentation, and repeated looks can undermine interpretation. Effects refer to the tested population, units, and time window. | [Larsen et al., *Statistical Challenges in Online Controlled Experiments* (2024 issue)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2023.2257237) · [Chou et al., *From More to Better* (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16335) |
| **Heterogeneous effects and uplift** · causal modeling and targeting | Estimate how treatment effects vary with pre-treatment attributes. Causal forests are one method. In customer retention, compare a policy that targets estimated incremental response with an incumbent targeting rule. | Subgroup effect estimates can be noisy, and the original design still determines whether the effects are identified. Evaluate the resulting policy on separate data with costs and constraints. | [Wager, *Causal Inference: A Statistical Learning Approach*, chapters 4–5 (2026 draft)](https://web.stanford.edu/~swager/causal_inf_book.pdf) |

## Turning estimates into decisions

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Recent reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Value of information** · decision theory | Estimate the improvement in expected value when a decision can use new information. Compare the benefit of buying data or running a pilot with its cost and the value lost while waiting. | The estimate depends on the available actions, outcome probabilities, consequences, and assumptions about how the evidence will influence action. | [Sadatsafavi et al., *Expected Value of Sample Information Calculations for Risk Prediction Model Validation* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0272989X251314010) |
| **Predictive to prescriptive analytics** · optimization framework | Use features and predicted uncertainty to choose an action under a business objective and constraints. For staffing, evaluate the schedule it produces using labor cost and service performance. | The objective needs to reflect relevant consequences. Test decisions under realistic constraints and changes in demand. | [Powell, *Sequential Decision Analytics and Modeling*, 2nd ed. (2026)](https://warrenpowell.org/sdamodeling/) · [Bertsimas, Everest, and Stoumpou, *Multimodal Prescriptive Deep Learning* (2026)](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=AwfWOCVLbJ) |
| **Newsvendor problem** · single-period inventory model | Choose an order quantity from the demand distribution by balancing shortage and overage costs. Under the standard linear cost model, the target demand quantile is shortage cost divided by the sum of shortage and overage costs. | The basic rule applies to a single period with the specified cost model. Replenishment during the period, shared capacity, or multi-period carryover changes the decision problem. | [Kocabıyıkoğlu et al., *Newsvendor Decisions Under Incomplete Information* (2024)](https://academic.oup.com/imaman/article/35/3/427/7642690) |

## Work, adoption, and governance

| Idea and type | Business use | Scope or limitation | Recent reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sociotechnical systems** · work-design theory | Study tools, roles, incentives, and coordination together. When adding AI to support work, examine who verifies drafts, handles exceptions, and bears additional review work. | The analysis depends on the actual work setting. Observe how the team adapts after deployment and measure both output quality and workload. | [Stanford Digital Economy Lab, *The Enterprise AI Playbook* (2026 deployment cases)](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/enterprise-ai-playbook/) |
| **Technology acceptance model** · information-systems theory | Use perceived usefulness and ease of use to formulate adoption hypotheses. Measure those perceptions alongside sustained usage for a new analytics tool. | Adoption intent, sustained use, and business value require separate measures. Access requirements, incentives, and implementation can affect each one. | [Nguyen et al., *Navigating Workplace AI Adoption* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1017/jmo.2026.10090) |
| **Dynamic capabilities** · strategy theory | Examine how a firm senses opportunities, commits resources, and reconfigures routines. An AI pilot that works in one team may demand reusable data, oversight, and staff skills before it can scale. | Specify which capabilities are observable and what outcomes would show improvement. Changes in performance require separate evidence. | [Wong and Ngai, *Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Operational Performance: The Role of Dynamic Capabilities* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.im.2025.104162) |
| **Task-based economics of automation** · economic framework | Describe work as a bundle of tasks. Identify which tasks technology performs, where people remain complementary, and which new tasks emerge, such as verification or evaluation. | A task-level productivity gain is one input into labor and organizational outcomes. Demand, reassignment, and newly created tasks also matter. | [Acemoglu, *The Simple Macroeconomics of AI* (2025 issue)](https://doi.org/10.1093/epolic/eiae042) |
| **Jagged technological frontier** · empirical finding and interpretive lens | Evaluate AI assistance at the task level. The field experiment found that consultants performed better on a set of tasks within the tested model's capabilities, and worse on a task selected outside them. | Where the boundary falls depends on the model, tools, users, and task. Test the actual workflow and its consequential failure cases. | [Dell’Acqua et al., *Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier* (2026 journal article)](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) |
| **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** · voluntary governance framework | Use Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage to assign owners, identify impacts, evaluate risks, and plan responses. Apply the generative AI profile to decisions about evidence, monitoring, and incidents. | The organization still has to define acceptable thresholds and demonstrate that its controls work in the use case. | [NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/AI/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) · [Generative AI Profile (2024)](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) |

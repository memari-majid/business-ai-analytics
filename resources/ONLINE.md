# Online resources for AI in business analytics

Reviewed November 18, 2026. The fastest route from the email campaign tutorial to business-school practice is to read the Wharton–Petco case, estimate campaign lift on the Hillstrom dataset, and use Anthropic's evaluation guide to test an AI analyst on the resulting decision. Browse the remaining resources by the question you want to answer.

## How businesses and business schools use AI

1. **Wharton–Petco email marketing case.** This public student-project write-up is the closest match to the tutorial. The team extracted text and image features, predicted click-through rates, and retrieved historical creative to generate new emails. Sketch the same workflow and specify the randomized comparison you would need to measure incremental purchases. The write-up describes the project and its reported impact. [Wharton case](https://ai-analytics.wharton.upenn.edu/student-programs/analytics-accelerator/optimizing-petcos-email-marketing-strategy-using-generative-ai/)
2. **MIT Sloan, The Cemex Journey to AI at Scale.** A real order-to-fulfillment case for teaching how data foundations, model monitoring, and business ownership keep AI working across markets. Identify who should approve the model, own the metric, and respond to drift. MIT's 2026 teaching page links to the open 2024 working paper. [Teaching objectives](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/teaching-resources-library/cemex-journey-to-ai-scale) · [Full case](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/175416)
3. **INSEAD, Taming GenAI to Automate Business Processes.** The publisher lists this as a free case. Use it to compare predictive AI, generative AI, rules, and human review within the same process. As a discussion exercise, mark which steps to automate and which decisions require a person. [Aloma case](https://publishing.insead.edu/case/taming-genai-automate-business-processes-aloma-example)
4. **Wharton AI & Analytics Accelerator.** The Fall 2026 briefs show what a business-school capstone is expected to deliver. Expedia's brief is on evaluating a multimodal travel agent, and RxSense's is on forecasting returns to enterprise AI investment. For one brief, draft a one-page plan with the decision owner, data requirements, a baseline, and success criteria. The briefs are public. The program works with sponsor data under its own access arrangements. [Current project briefs and past cases](https://ai-analytics.wharton.upenn.edu/for-students/ai-analytics-accelerator/)
5. **Eccles Marketing Text Analytics.** The Spring 2026 syllabus pairs text analysis, primarily in R, with a Yelp restaurant review project. Its project brief shows how students move from sentiment and topics to recommendations on service, menus, or promotions. Use it to design a short exercise that asks students to justify a business decision from text evidence. [Eccles syllabus and project brief](https://class-tools.app.utah.edu/syllabus/1264/12473/MKTG%2B4640%2BSyllabus.pdf#page=8)

## Datasets and notebooks for practice

6. **Hillstrom email experiment.** The closest real-data follow-on to the synthetic tutorial. This historical retail experiment randomized about 64,000 customers to one of two promotional emails or no email. Start with each email's incremental conversions and spend against the common control. Then test whether an uplift model improves a targeting policy on a held-out set. [Dataset access](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/hillstrom) · [Challenge organizer’s follow-up and winning analysis](https://blog.minethatdata.com/2008/05/best-answer-e-mail-analytics-challenge.html)
7. **Criteo Uplift Prediction Dataset.** Randomized advertising data with assignment, exposure, visits, and conversions. Compare an intent-to-treat estimate using assignment with a simple contrast using actual exposure. Use the gap to discuss why observed exposure can select a different population. The publisher provides license terms and download links. [Criteo AI Lab](https://ailab.criteo.com/criteo-uplift-prediction-dataset/)
8. **UCI Online Retail II.** Two years of transaction records from a UK online retailer. Build a seasonal baseline for weekly sales or an RFM customer segmentation, then evaluate on a later period. Transactions can support forecasting and descriptive analysis. An incremental effect requires additional identification evidence. [UCI dataset](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii)
9. **CFPB Consumer Complaint Database.** Publicly downloadable financial-services complaints include consumer narratives where publication was authorized. Use a sample with available narratives to compare a simple classifier with an LLM on complaint routing. Manually verify ambiguous records and read the CFPB's coverage notes before interpreting counts as market trends. [Public data and coverage](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/)
10. **Google Meridian notebooks.** Run the public sample-data demo through model checks, channel response curves, and budget optimization. Change an ROI prior or compare the calibration demo with your original recommendation. Results depend on the quality of the data, model assumptions, and calibration. [Getting-started notebook](https://developers.google.com/meridian/notebook/meridian-getting-started) · [All example notebooks](https://developers.google.com/meridian/notebook)

## Test AI workflows and analytics decisions

11. **Microsoft Research, Patterns of Trustworthy Experimentation.** Start with the metric taxonomy for business outcomes, diagnostics, data quality, and guardrails. Add sample-ratio mismatch and an outcome-maturity check to the campaign analysis before looking at lift. [During-experiment guide](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/patterns-of-trustworthy-experimentation-during-experiment-stage/) · [Sample-ratio mismatch](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/diagnosing-sample-ratio-mismatch-in-a-b-testing/)
12. **Anthropic, Demystifying Evals for AI Agents.** An engineering guide to scoring the result and inspecting the trace. Start with a few known failure cases, such as a wrong denominator, an unauthorized action, and a missing cost input. Use code to grade exact arithmetic and a human reviewer for the reasoning. Report pass rates across repeated runs. [2026 guide](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
13. **Inspect AI.** An open-source framework from the UK AI Security Institute and Meridian Labs. Use the custom scorer example to build an evaluation from your own task samples and inspect how the model reached the recommendation. Run the same cases against two configurations and compare task-level failures. [Scoring documentation](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/scoring.html) · [Log viewer](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/log-viewer)
14. **Argo-Bench.** The October 2026 preprint and released benchmark evaluate agents on forecasting, finance, and other workflows inside a simulated delivery marketplace. Read the examples and choose one to adapt as a small, interpretable exercise. Grade the business result and the selected metric, alongside the agent's explanation. [Paper and release links](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02122)
15. **BusinessCaseBench.** This 2026 preprint scores model answers to questions drawn from business cases using instructor-derived rubrics. Use the design as inspiration for an original vignette with a scoring rubric. Require learners to defend one assumption and adapt their recommendation when it changes. The benchmark measures model answers to cases. [Project page](https://business-ai-benchmark.github.io/) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16057)
16. **NIST Generative AI Profile.** Translate the public risk-management guidance into a short review for a proposed AI feature. Assign an owner for evaluation, define evidence required before launch, and specify what will be monitored and escalated after launch. [NIST AI 600-1](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence)

## Follow new research

17. **Scite.** Follow supporting, contrasting, and mentioning citations to a seed paper and inspect the actual citation context. Start with a DOI from the campaign tutorial and record what each subsequent study actually tests. Smart Citation labels are an aid to reading, and some features depend on your plan or institutional access. [Scite features](https://scite.ai/features) · [Access options](https://scite.ai/pricing)
18. **Wharton Generative AI Labs.** Follow the research archive for AI in learning, collaboration, and knowledge work. The October 2026 Wharton adoption report is useful for understanding what surveyed organizations report doing with AI. Pair those self-reports with a field experiment when estimating actual effects. [Research and insights](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/) · [2026 adoption report](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/special-report/2026-ai-adoption-report/)
19. **Stanford HAI.** Start with the 2026 AI Index's Economy chapter for an annual snapshot of adoption, productivity, and work. Follow the November 2026 AI100 panel report for the broader implications for organizations and education. Use the underlying sources when turning a headline statistic into a teaching claim. [AI Index Economy chapter](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy) · [2026 AI100 report](https://ai100.stanford.edu/reports/2026-report)
20. **NBER Digital Economics and Artificial Intelligence.** The Summer Institute 2026 program links to emerging research on firms, labor, and decision-making. Pick a study with an explicit comparison group and trace the business outcome, assignment unit, and time horizon before using it in a lecture. [2026 program and papers](https://www.nber.org/conferences/si-2026-digital-economics-and-artificial-intelligence)
